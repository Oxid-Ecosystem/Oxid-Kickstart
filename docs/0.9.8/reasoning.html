Reasoning (OWL 2 EL⊥)

State a little, and Oxid-DB derives the rest. Its built-in reasoner closes your class hierarchy, propagates types to individuals, and rejects writes that contradict your ontology, all in guaranteed polynomial time, so it can run on every commit.

Since 0.9.7 Version 0.9.7 turns on length-2 role chains and transitivity by default: a chain axiom r∘s ⊑ t is reasoned via the CR8 completion rule, and Trans(r) desugars to the self-chain r∘r ⊑ r. Role axioms are now authorable live through POST /actions/add-axiom with the new kind "subproperty" (previously file-import only), and ObjectPropertyDomain(r, C) is now reasoned as the existential subclass ∃r.⊤ ⊑ C. It stays an honest OWL 2 EL⊥ classifier: length-3-and-longer chains and role range restrictions remain excluded. See the Changelog for the full 0.9.6 → 0.9.7 difference.

What reasoning gives you

An ontology lets you say a few things and have the engine work out the consequences. You declare that Pilsner is a subclass of Beer, that Beer is a subclass of Beverage, and that Heineken is a Pilsner. You never say “Heineken is a Beer”: the reasoner infers it, and also that Heineken is a Beverage and an owl:Thing. Ask FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A Beer and Heineken comes back, even though it was only ever asserted as a Pilsner.

Three kinds of derivation matter in practice:

  • Hierarchy closure: every class learns its complete set of superclasses, transitively.
  • Type propagation: every individual gains membership in all of its classes' superclasses, so a query for a broad class returns members declared only against narrow subclasses.
  • Contradiction detection: if your facts force something impossible (an individual in two classes you declared mutually exclusive), the reasoner reports it, and the write gate can reject it.

Because this runs inside the database rather than in a separate batch job, the same inferred facts are visible to vector search, to the hybrid query planner, and to the write path.

OWL 2 EL⊥ in plain terms

Oxid-DB's reasoner implements OWL 2 EL⊥: the EL family of description logic plus ⊥ (bottom). It is a strict subset of the OWL 2 EL profile, not the whole of it. Here is what each piece means:

EL is the description logic built around three operators: conjunction (“a thing that is both A and B”, written A ⊓ B), qualified existential restriction (“a thing that has some part that is an Engine”, written ∃hasPart.Engine), and the top class owl:Thing (⊤). That is deliberately modest: there is no negation, no disjunction (“A or B”), no universal restriction (“all parts are …”), and no counting.

⊥ (bottom, owl:Nothing) is the empty class: the class nothing can belong to. It is what makes disjointness expressible: declaring Beer and Wine disjoint is internally the statement that anything which is both a Beer and a Wine belongs to ⊥. If some individual is then forced into both, it is forced into the empty class, a contradiction the reasoner catches. Without ⊥ you could describe hierarchies but never say “these two things can't overlap.”

Why EL is fast (PTime) EL was chosen precisely because classification is polynomial-time (PTime): the reasoner saturates facts with a fixed set of completion rules rather than searching over possible models. Adding negation, disjunction, or universal restrictions would push the logic into ALC and beyond, where classification becomes exponential and needs a fundamentally different (tableau) engine. Polynomial time is what lets Oxid-DB reason on every write without an adversarial ontology stalling a commit. The expressivity ceiling is a deliberate architectural choice, grounded in Baader, Brandt & Lutz's “Pushing the EL Envelope” (IJCAI 2005) and the W3C OWL 2 EL profile.

What it infers

The classifier normalizes every axiom into EL normal form and then saturates with a small set of completion rules. The inferences you can rely on:

InferenceExample (beverages)Rule
Transitive subclass closure: every class gets its full superclass set. Pilsner ⊑ Beer, Beer ⊑ Beverage ⇒ Pilsner ⊑ Beverage. CR1 crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs
Type propagation to individuals: asserted types flow up the hierarchy. Heineken : Pilsner ⇒ Heineken : Beer, : Beverage, : owl:Thing. CR1 over ABox crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs
Conjunction: membership in two classes yields a third. Beer ⊓ Belgian ⊑ BelgianBeer: something in both is a BelgianBeer. CR2 crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs
Existential propagation: reasons through ∃r.C restrictions and their fillers. Beer ⊑ ∃servedIn.Glass; a subclass of Glass still satisfies the filler. CR3 / CR4 crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs
Disjointness → unsatisfiable: overlap of disjoint classes collapses to ⊥. Beer ⊥ Wine; an individual asserted both is a consistency violation. CR2 via ⊓ ⊑ ⊥; CR5 crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs
Single sub-property (role hierarchy): a fact on a sub-property is inherited by its super-property. brewedBy ⊑ producedBy: x brewedBy y is also seen as x producedBy y for reasoning. CR7 crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs
Length-2 role chains and transitivity: composing two edges concludes a third; a transitive role closes over itself. hasParent∘hasParent ⊑ hasGrandparent: two hasParent steps infer a hasGrandparent edge. Trans(partOf): partOf(x,y) and partOf(y,z) infer partOf(x,z). CR8 crates/oxd-reasoner/src/classify.rs

Length-2 role chains and transitivity (CR8) are reasoned by default as of 0.9.7. Single sub-property inheritance (CR7) has shipped since 0.9.6: a single SubObjectPropertyOf edge is reasoned. Length-3-and-longer chains are still fenced off; see Role chains and transitivity and Limitations.

Role chains and transitivity (CR8)

A length-2 role chain axiom r∘s ⊑ t reads “compose role r then role s, and conclude role t”. As of 0.9.7 the classifier reasons these through the CR8 completion rule. With hasParent∘hasParent ⊑ hasGrandparent, two consecutive hasParent edges infer a hasGrandparent edge; a query along hasGrandparent then returns pairs you never asserted directly.

Transitivity is the special self-chain case. Trans(r) desugars to r∘r ⊑ r, so with Trans(partOf), the facts partOf(x,y) and partOf(y,z) infer partOf(x,z). Because a chain can also feed type propagation, new class memberships (not just new relationships) can be drawn along chains and transitive roles.

On by default, and a behaviour change on upgrade CR8 is on by default in 0.9.7 (the completion gate passed on 2026-09-09). Because chains and transitive roles now fire, upgrading can infer relationships and class memberships that earlier releases did not. To opt out, set the environment variable OXD_REASONER_ROLE_CHAINS=0. With chains opted out, any stored chain axioms are reported loudly as inert by GET /reasoner/fragment, never silently ignored.

This stays within OWL 2 EL⊥. Chains of length 3 or longer are still fenced off (pending binarization), and role range restrictions, role equivalence, functional and inverse roles, nominals, and concrete domains remain out of scope. See Limitations for the full list.

Authoring a role hierarchy live

You no longer need a file import to add a sub-property (role hierarchy) edge. POST /actions/add-axiom gains the kind "subproperty" alongside the existing "subclass", "disjoint", and "equivalent" kinds. It is a governed write: the axiom commits only if the ontology stays coherent, otherwise the endpoint returns the EL⊥ verdict without mutating anything.

json
{ "kind": "subproperty", "sub": "https://ex.org/prop/brewedBy", "sup": "https://ex.org/prop/producedBy" }

Role chains are not authorable through this action; author chain axioms via file import. The same live sub-property capability is mirrored in the SDKs (Node AddAxiomRequest with kind "subproperty"; PHP AddAxiomRequest::subproperty($sub, $sup)) and in the MCP add_axiom tool, and role axioms now round-trip through OKF export under the x-oxid-role-axioms key. GET /reasoner/fragment reports a stored length-1 role hierarchy as in-fragment and reasoned.

Domain restrictions are reasoned

ObjectPropertyDomain(r, C) is now reasoned. On import it translates to ∃r.⊤ ⊑ C, which is already in the reasoner’s normal form, so asserting an r edge types its subject into the domain class C. Role range restrictions remain excluded.

Running classification

Classification is triggered automatically the first time you query after a change, but you can run it explicitly, for example to check consistency before relying on the result. It returns whether the ontology is consistent (no ABox contradiction) and coherent (no class forced empty), plus a violation count.

curl -s -X POST https://85cfad134a4a.oxid-db.com/classify
with OxidDB("https://85cfad134a4a.oxid-db.com") as db:
    result = db.classify()
    print("consistent:", result.get("consistent"))
const c = await db.classify();
if (!c.consistent) {
  console.error(`inconsistent: ${c.violations} violation(s)`, c.violation_witnesses);
} else {
  console.log("consistent", c.coherent ? "and coherent" : "");
}
cls, err := client.Classify(ctx)
if err != nil {
	log.Fatalf("classify: %v", err)
}
log.Printf("consistent=%v coherent=%v violations=%d",
	cls.Consistent, cls.Coherent, cls.Violations)
Ops.ClassifyResponse cl = oxid.classify();
if (cl.ok() && cl.consistent()) {
    // reasoner ran; inferred classes are now available on individuals
}
$classify = $oxid->classify();                       // POST /classify
printf("consistent=%s coherent=%s violations=%d\n",
    $classify->consistent ? 'true' : 'false',
    $classify->coherent   ? 'true' : 'false',
    $classify->violations,
);
ClassifyResponse res = await oxid.ClassifyAsync();
Console.WriteLine($"ok={res.Ok} consistent={res.Consistent} " +
                  $"coherent={res.Coherent} violations={res.Violations}");

Once classified, a plain IS-A query returns inferred members, not just asserted ones. The query below returns every individual the reasoner places under Beer, including those declared only as Pilsner or Stout:

curl -s -X POST https://85cfad134a4a.oxid-db.com/query \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"query":"FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A Beer LIMIT 20"}'
with OxidDB("https://85cfad134a4a.oxid-db.com") as db:
    page = db.query("FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A Beer LIMIT 20")
    for hit in page.get("results", []):
        print(hit["iri"])
const page = await db.query({ query: "FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A Beer LIMIT 20" });
console.log(page.results.map((r) => r.iri));
console.log(page.total_count);
res, err := client.Query(ctx, oxid.QueryRequest{
	Query: "FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A " + oxid.Quote("Beer"),

}
log.Printf("matched %d (showing %d)", res.TotalCount, len(res.Results))
for _, hit := range res.Results {
	log.Println(" -", hit.IRI)
}
Query.QueryResponse page = oxid.query(
        Query.QueryRequest.of("FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A " + Oxql.quote("Beer")));
for (Query.QueryHit hit : page.results()) {
    System.out.println(hit.iri());
}
String next = page.nextCursor();              // null on the last page
use OxidDb\Client\Model\QueryRequest;

$page = $oxid->query(new QueryRequest('FIND ?x WHERE ?x IS-A Beer LIMIT 20'));
echo "total: {$page->totalCount}\n";
foreach ($page->results as $hit) {

}

{

}
IS-A is inference, not a lookup ?x IS-A C returns every instance of C and every instance of every subclass of C, transitively, driven by the classification closure. This is the difference between a reasoner and a plain type filter.

Consistency & disjointness

Disjointness is how you turn an ontology into a data-quality gate. Declare that nothing can be both a Beer and a Wine:

curl -s -X POST https://85cfad134a4a.oxid-db.com/disjoint \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"a":"Beer","b":"Wine"}'
with OxidDB("https://85cfad134a4a.oxid-db.com") as db:
    db.add_disjoint("Beer", "Wine")
// Nothing is both a Beer and a Wine.
await db.addDisjoint({ a: "Beer", b: "Wine" });
// Beer and Wine are disjoint: nothing can be both.
if err := client.AddDisjoint(ctx, oxid.DisjointRequest{A: "Beer", B: "Wine"}); err != nil {
	log.Fatalf("disjoint: %v", err)
}
// Beer and Wine are disjoint
oxid.addDisjoint(new Mutation.DisjointRequest("Beer", "Wine"));
use OxidDb\Client\Model\DisjointRequest;

$oxid->addDisjoint(new DisjointRequest('Beer', 'Wine'));   // POST /disjoint: Beer ⊓ Wine = ⊥
// A beverage cannot be both a Beer and a Wine.
await oxid.AddDisjointAsync(new DisjointRequest("Beer", "Wine"));

Now suppose an individual is forced into both classes: say Heineken is asserted a Beer and, through some other subclass, also inferred a Wine. The overlap lands in ⊥, and the next classification reports the ontology inconsistent: consistent is false, violations is non-zero, and violation_witnesses names the offending individuals. A /classify response for a healthy ontology looks like this:

json
{ "ok": true, "consistent": true, "coherent": true, "violations": 0, "violation_witnesses": [], "unsatisfiable": [] }

Two verdicts are reported and they are independent:

consistent
ABox integrity: no individual is (asserted or inferred to be) a member of two disjoint classes. This is what a governed write cares about.
coherent
TBox satisfiability: no named class is entailed to be empty (⊑ ⊥). An incoherent TBox can still be consistent: an unsatisfiable class simply has no members.

To vet a change before committing it, use the speculative endpoint POST /consistency/check with a set of candidate assertions: it returns the EL⊥ verdict without writing anything. Compound writes via POST /entities accept a gate: true flag that runs the consistency gate before commit and refuses the write if it would break integrity.

Limitations (be honest)

This is OWL 2 EL⊥, a strict subset of OWL 2 EL

The reasoner is sound and complete for the fragment it implements, but that fragment is deliberately smaller than full OWL 2 EL. Do not assume broader OWL coverage than is listed below.

  • Length-2 chains and transitivity are reasoned; longer chains are not. A SubObjectPropertyOf with a length-2 chain body (r∘s ⊑ t) and transitive properties (which desugar to r∘r ⊑ r) are reasoned by default via CR8 as of 0.9.7. Chains of length 3 or longer are still fenced off (pending binarization): they are stored but not reasoned, and skipped loudly so the fragment report (GET /reasoner/fragment) counts them, never silently. If you opt out with OXD_REASONER_ROLE_CHAINS=0, stored chain axioms are likewise reported as inert rather than ignored.
  • Defined-class / complex-equivalence gaps. Some complex definitional equivalences (e.g. C ≡ ∃r.D) and a few ABox completion rules are not fully covered. An equivalence with a complex side may not round-trip both directions.
  • Domain is reasoned; range is not. ObjectPropertyDomain(r, C) is reasoned as of 0.9.7 (it translates to ∃r.⊤ ⊑ C), so an r edge types its subject into C. Role range restrictions, role equivalence, and functional / inverse roles are not reasoning rules.
  • Out of profile entirely: negation, disjunction (“A or B”), universal restrictions (∀), cardinality, nominals, and concrete domains are outside EL by design. Adding them would forfeit polynomial-time classification. On import, class expressions using them collapse rather than being reasoned.

What to do instead: model part-whole and relational facts you need today with sub-properties (CR7), length-2 chains and transitivity (CR8), domain restrictions, and explicit EL axioms; before trusting inferences on an imported ontology that uses richer OWL, check GET /reasoner/fragment to see exactly which axiom shapes were dropped.
